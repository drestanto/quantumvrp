Project: Quantum Optimisation for the Vehicle Routing Problem

proposal

Mark: 17.5/30
Comment:

Question Marker1Marker2
Organisation of proposal and clarity of expressionWhile there is some interesting material in the proposal, it lacks a clear flow. It seems to jump between different topics (definitions, related work, research challenges, approach) at different points in the report, which makes it very hard to follow.The organisation of this proposal needs more work to make it easier to follow. Some sections and subsections should be renamed and reorganised. For example, Section 3 has not clearly described and discussed the motivation and key challenges of VRP as indicated by the section title. Section 6 contains the main technical content, but some subsections such as 6.12, 6.13 need more context. The discussions in them are not well connected with the preceding subsections. Section 8 should be renamed, instead of having two sections on Methodology. The writing also requires a significant improvement to make the content easy to understand and cohesive. It would be important to define abbreviations (e.g. TSP, TDS), add captions to figures and tables, correct format errors (e.g. some subheadings didn’t start a new line) and citation errors in subsection 7.2. Including some figures can be helpful to explain the methodology, e.g. graph models for VRP, and the structures/workflow of different approaches.
Research aim, objectives, and planThe project addresses an interesting challenge. From the report, it is rather hard to follow the plan for the project.The research objectives and plan are clear. Although the methodology section compares a few options for each step, more discussion on the performance criteria is required to understand how to evaluate different methods. Eventually, this will be critical to assess whether the project has achieved all the objectives.
Review of the relevant literatureWhile the report refers to a range of relevant literature, it is hard to follow how the specific research questions follow from the gaps in the existing research.Section 5 Literature Review has covered the quantum approximate optimisation for VRP and several potential methods, but there should be some discussions on the limitations of the current methods and research gaps.

report

Mark: 62/100
Comment:

Question Marker1Marker2
Organisation of Thesis and Clarity of ExpressionThe thesis has several organisational structure issues. Although the flow of information in the earlier section of the thesis (abstract and introduction) is well-aligned with the purpose of conveying the message, the transition later on becomes unclear, as content is added without proper chapter introductions. The reader gets confused whether they are part of the introduction or another chapter. Hence, the overall structure MUST be revised, with clear chapters. In fact, we don't refer to section 2, 3, etc we need to refer to their chapters. The thesis was very hard to follow. In addition, There exist some typos and style issues, e.g., - The vechicle Routing Problem (VRP). - Redefinition of the acronyms . There are also mismatches between the form of the citation format, and different styles have been used. Please stick to one format. - the location of figures could have been improved. - there exist some typos across the thesis, e.g., in section 4.2.1The overall structure is fairly standard, and the summaries at the end of each section are useful. The English expression is good, but some sections are hard to understand because of domain-specific terminology that is not clearly defined, such as different types of quantum gates.
Grasp of the problem and review of the relevant literatureThe problem statement was clear in both the abstract and the introduction, and although the relevant studies were mentioned in Section 2, the reader was confused about whether they were part of the background or the literature review. Hence, it must be revised accordingly. Overall, the relevant studies were mentioned. The literature review is not adequate for a general CS audience, which makes it hard to assess whether the student has an adequate grasp of the problem. I realise that it is hard to describe quantum computing within the scope of a thesis, but it is reasonable to expect it to be done better than it has been. For example, Section 2.4.1 has one displayed equation which juxtaposes a sequence of U_M and U_C functions, but does not explain what juxtaposition means. Normally, it means multiplication, but since the functions are all exponential, the product of the U_M and U_C functions collapses to a single exponential, which seems inappropriate here. The meaning of bra/ket notation should be defined. I am familiar with linear operators where a and c are complex functions, but not with in 2.4.1, or with |phi(x)> in 2.7.2 where phi(x) = |psi(x)> is a linear operator. Section 2.7.3 should cite examples of publications using quantum-inspired classical algorithms. Section 4.1.1 suggests that a search-based solver found a solution (26.035km) that is shorter than the exhaustive-search (26.039 km). That is impossible, barring round-off errors, but was not explained as such.
Research method (including data collection and analysis where relevant)The study relies on publicly available data from the City of Casey and relevant information is given in the introduction. But chapters 3 and 4 could have been reorganised in a way to separate the implementation details (e.g., section 4.2.1) from the overall experiment design. It initially talks about some hyperparameters and then dives into the design. The connection to Chapter 3 is also not clear, so, reorganising the thesis is a must. The parameters and relevant stuff should be part of the next chapter, and the focus should be purely on the design and research method, particularly, starting from section 4.2.6, as it shows the core of the research method. It is good to have a concrete case study. Although the role of the problem encoding is central to this thesis, its role in determining things like the smoothness of the error surface are not discussed, and the description of how it affects the complexity of the quantum program (in terms of, say, number of gates per layer) is not clear. Similarly, the benefits of increasing the depth are not clear. The formula for the cost of invalid strings in point 3 of 3.4.5 always evaluates to 0, since it is summed only over the valid string. It should presumably be summed over all bit-strings. When calculating the optimality ratio, it is not clear why the expected quantum solution is used, rather than the best valid quantum solution.
Presentation of resultsThe Section/Chapter starts with a summary of results, which must have been put at the end. It does not add value in the beginning, as the reader would want to see how the proposed approach would work and then have a summary. The figures and results are not well-presented, as it feels like in section 5.3, the number of faults could have been presented in a smaller table or even by reducing the outputs to a range format, as the following paragraph confirms this. In addition, the reader is confused about the use of "table" with different formats vs outputs in sections. Please maintain one format, similar to Table 2. The figures also could have been smaller, and the allocated space could have been used for a more detailed discussion (in addition to the next chapter regarding discussion) The other issue is that subsections could have been discussed initially at the beginning of the chapter, providing a higher level of what to expect instead of a summary of results. In addition, Section 6 could have been merged into this chapter, in its relevant sections. Lastly, the reader is unsure whether the limitations and future work should be included here as a separate section in the conclusion chapter, which is dedicated to this. Also, in chapter 6, the way comparison is conducted with the related works is not complete. The reader would expect to see that the actual comparison is done rather than a conceptual discussion. Were the relevant studies implemented and tested, or is it purely based on a discussion? Hence, a revision of the thesis is strongly recommended. Table 1 doesn't match the text before it. The numbers of optimal solutions found were 4 and 13 in the text, but 8 and 4 in the table. It is not clear what "invalid percentage" means, but the numbers look implausibly high, given the success rates. The unnumbered tables in Sections 5.2 and 5.3 could have been presented much better. For example, it would have been clearer to have the optimality ratios for the two encodings on a single line per problem ID. Currently, the only way to interpret the last column is to look all the way back to the second column (encoding). The striking peak in Fig 3 seems not to be discussed. Since naive_adjacency has twice the fraction of optimal answers as node_visit_time, it seems that that is the better encoding when there are enough qubits for it, but the thesis seems to say otherwise. In Table 3, it is not clear why the actual cost depends on the QAOA formulation. Does this mean "estimated cost"? It is not clear why the constraint "no duplicate positions" is called "local", since it depends on all nodes' data. It is not clear why it is concluded that performance plateaus after p=3~5. Figure 5 seems fairly flat throughout, with a notable degradation at p=4, right in the middle of the recommended range 3~5. The discussion in 6.2 is good.
Conclusion and suggestions for future workThe way the conclusion and the future work are explained is confusing, as there are multiple subsections that, to some extent, are disjoint. The recommendation here is to reduce them and place them in the most relevant sections that are logically and conceptually relevant. In addition, for some future works, our claims should have been better supported with some references or case studies on why it matters, rather than just having a discussion, as the research's direction could have been better directed toward future contributions based on the developed tool. As noted above, some of the conclusions do not seem to be supported by the numerical results. The future work is not very specific, but seems to point in good directions.
ContributionThe research project has led to an open-source software project, which demonstrates the intentions and efforts to make it available to the research community and is the first comprehensive, experimental comparison of qubit allocation strategies for solving quantum VRP tested on real quantum hardware. However, the work could have benefited from better error mitigation as stated in Chapter/Section 7. The contribution of performing the first systematic encoding comparison seems worthwhile. Other contributions, such as providing parameter guidelines, would be more substantial if they were better supported by the data.
CommentsThis thesis is somewhat adequate, but it highlights the need for further work to grasp the study's principles and outcomes. Explaining quantum computing is quite challenging, and while efforts have been made, improvements are still needed.This thesis is adequate, although it suggests room for improvement in understanding of the principles and results of the study. It is challenging to describe a field such as quantum computing to a mainstream computer scientist, and a good effort has been made to do that.
